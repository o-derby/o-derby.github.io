Research Workspace

A local-first research workspace · In progress, Aug 2026

This is a local-first macOS research workspace, the reference manager I want for my history thesis, built as something closer to Zotero with an AI layer than to a chat window that happens to read PDFs. It's in progress; what follows separates what works today from what's still ahead, because I think that distinction is the honest way to show unfinished work.

The foundation milestone is working. Projects and imported PDFs are stored locally in SQLite behind a versioned migration with foreign keys, so the schema can move without me hand-editing a database. Multi-file PDF import copies sources into managed local storage and runs SHA-256 hashing to catch duplicates, which matters more than it sounds when the same paper reaches you three times under three filenames. There's a project-filtered and all-source library view, and a PDF.js reader with paging and zoom built into the app.

Still ahead: text extraction and search, secure provider credential storage, the AI chat itself, citation validation, annotations, notes, and export. The architecture doc plans grounded question-answering with retrieval and citation validation rather than open-ended generation, which is the part I actually care about getting right. A research tool that produces a confident citation to something that doesn't exist is worse than no tool. For a history thesis, it's the one failure mode that would make the whole thing untrustworthy.

Building it local-first was a deliberate call. Research notes and unpublished source material are exactly the kind of thing I don't want sitting on someone else's server by default, and it means the reader and library work with no network at all.

Stack: Tauri 2 · React · TypeScript · Rust · SQLite · PDF.js
